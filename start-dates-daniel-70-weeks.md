# Possible Start Dates for Daniel's 70 Weeks of Years

The prophecy of "70 weeks of years" (or "Seventy Sevens") from Daniel 9:24-27 is subject to various interpretations, with several potential starting points being proposed. Below is a list of five commonly suggested dates:

---

## Proposed Start Dates

### 1. **457 BCE**
- **Event:** Decree by Artaxerxes I to Ezra to restore Jerusalem (see Ezra 7:7-26).
- **Details:** Popular among Protestant interpretations, including Adventist and Dispensationalist traditions.
- **Calculation:** Leads to around AD 27, corresponding with the beginning of Jesus's public ministry.

---

### 2. **445 BCE**
- **Event:** Decree of Artaxerxes I to Nehemiah for rebuilding Jerusalem's walls (Nehemiah 2:1-8).
- **Details:** Highlighted in Sir Robert Anderson's book "The Coming Prince."
- **Calculation:** Ends around AD 32–33 using a "prophetic year" of 360 days, placing it close to the time of Jesus' crucifixion.

---

### 3. **538 BCE**
- **Event:** Decree of Cyrus the Great allowing Jews to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the Temple (Ezra 1:1-4).
- **Details:** Often linked to the earlier Jewish return under Persian rule but results in earlier fulfillments.

---

### 4. **520 BCE**
- **Event:** Decree under Darius I to resume Temple rebuilding after interruptions (Ezra 6:1-12).
- **Details:** Acknowledged but less commonly cited for Daniel's prophecy.

---

### 5. **609 BCE**
- **Event:** Suggested desolation-related starting point following the death of King Josiah.
- **Details:** A minority perspective aligning with other events in Jewish history.

---

### Summary:
The choice between these dates depends on how one interprets the decree to "restore and rebuild Jerusalem" and the historical context. The most widely accepted start dates are **457 BCE** and **445 BCE**.
